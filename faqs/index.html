FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Answers to the questions we hear most often about fertility treatment at Birmingham IVF.

Getting started

What should I expect during my initial consultation?

You’ll meet Dr Datta, who reviews your history, circumstances and any other medical issues. A fertility-focused ultrasound with colour Doppler and 3D imaging is done in the same visit, and together you agree a personalised treatment plan.

Do I need a GP referral?

No. You can refer yourself by booking directly. If you have a GP letter or previous test results, bring them along, but they aren’t needed to book.

What is the success rate at Birmingham IVF?

As a new clinic we can’t yet give a reliable success rate. Once enough patients have been treated, our rates will be published here and on the HFEA website.

Mild and Natural cycle IVF

What is Mild and Natural cycle IVF and how does it differ from conventional IVF?

Mild and Natural cycle IVF uses a lower dose of FSH, generally 150 IU a day or less, so that only the healthier follicles grow. Conventional IVF uses higher doses to collect more eggs. Mild stimulation gives fewer eggs but is safer, gentler and more affordable.

Is Mild and Natural cycle IVF as effective as conventional IVF?

Randomised controlled trials show similar live birth rates per fresh cycle and similar cumulative live birth rates.

What is OHSS and how is the risk reduced?

Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome is an exaggerated response to stimulation drugs. It is rarely serious, though severe cases may need hospital care. Modern protocols, such as an agonist trigger and freezing all embryos, almost eliminate significant OHSS. Mild stimulation lowers the risk further, and every cycle is closely monitored.

Will I produce enough eggs?

The number of eggs depends mainly on your age and ovarian reserve. If your ovaries can’t produce enough eggs in one cycle for the best chance of success, we offer a package of multiple cycles.

Your circumstances

I’m over 40. Is this approach suitable for me?

We offer treatment with your own eggs up to age 46 where it is reasonable, for those who want to try before considering donor eggs or who won’t consider them. Live birth with your own eggs after 46 is almost unheard of, so we don’t consider it ethical to offer treatment beyond that age.

I’ve been told I’m a poor responder. Can you help?

Yes. Poor responders get an individualised plan, and if your ovaries produce too few eggs we usually offer multiple cycles to make up for it.

I have PCOS. Is this approach right for me?

Women with PCOS have a higher risk of OHSS with conventional IVF. Gentler stimulation reduces that risk, and your protocol is tailored to how you respond.

Can I freeze embryos?

Yes. Surplus good-quality embryos can be vitrified (frozen) for a future transfer. Survival rates after vitrification are above 99%.

Costs

Are medication costs included in the IVF packages?

No. Medication costs depend on your protocol and aren’t part of the package. They average £800 – £1,000 per IVF cycle, and you’ll get a full breakdown of medication prices.

When do we need to make the payment?

The full cost of treatment is paid at or before your treatment consultation with the nurses.
